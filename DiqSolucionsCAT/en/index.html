How can I help you?

01

Know where you stand

Financial control and reporting

It is not about producing more reports. It is about knowing what information you need to steer better.

  • Sales and margin trends.
  • Profitability by product, service, customer or business unit.
  • Cost trends.
  • Variances against budget.
  • Key financial indicators.
  • Economic and financial position.

Clear information to know what is really going on.

02

Know where you are heading

Financial planning and forecasting

Running a company looking only in the rear-view mirror limits your decisions.

  • Annual budgets.
  • Forecasts and projections.
  • Financial models.
  • Scenario analysis.
  • Sales, cost and results forecasts.
  • Variance analysis.
  • Financial planning.

Because a good decision starts long before it is taken.

03

Know how much cash you need

Treasury and cash flow

A company can be profitable and still have cash problems.

  • Cash forecasts.
  • Cash flow.
  • Control of collections and payments.
  • Working capital needs.
  • Liquidity planning.
  • Funding needs.

The question is not only how much you earn. It is how much cash you will have when you need it.

04

Know which decision to take

Finance in the service of business decisions

Some decisions can change a company's course. A new investment. A hire. A price change. A new line of business. An acquisition. An outsourcing.

  • Assess the alternatives economically before deciding.
  • Put the numbers on the table to compare different scenarios.

Not to tell you which decision to take. But so that you can take it with better information.

05

Secure the resources to grow

Funding and relations with banks and investors

When a company looks for funding, it needs much more than a profit and loss account. It needs to explain its business, its forecasts and its capacity to generate resources.

  • Financial models.
  • Business plans.
  • Forecasts.
  • Financial information.
  • Presentations for financial institutions.
  • Information for partners and investors.
  • Funding needs analysis.

Financial information is also a negotiating tool.

Frequently asked questions

Is it the same as hiring a CFO?

Not exactly. A Fractional CFO or external finance director takes on the functions of financial leadership with a commitment adapted to the company's needs, without having to add a full-time position.

How many hours will my company need?

There is no standard commitment. It depends on your company's situation, complexity and goals. First we analyse what you need and then we define the right level of commitment.

Will you work with our accounting team?

Yes. Financial leadership can work together with the advisory firm, the head of administration or the internal accounting team. The aim is for financial information to be reliable, timely and useful for management.

Is it a service only for large companies?

No. It is especially designed for companies that have reached a level of complexity where they need a more advanced financial function, even though it doesn't yet make sense to add a full-time CFO.

Can you help if we have cash problems?

Yes. We can analyse the cash position, identify liquidity needs and build forecasts that let you anticipate different scenarios.

Can you prepare a budget and a financial plan?

Yes. We can develop budgets, forecasts, financial models and scenarios adapted to the company's needs.

Can you help us find funding?

I can help you prepare the financial information, models, forecasts and plans needed for conversations with banks, investors, partners or other sources of funding.

Can we start with a specific project?

Yes. You don't have to begin with a recurring service. We can start with one specific need, review the result and, from there, decide whether it makes sense to continue with ongoing financial support.
